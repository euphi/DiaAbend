DiaAbend ("slide night") shows the photos on your home server on the big screen. That's all it does.

There are surprisingly few plain photo viewers for the local network. DLNA doesn't hold up with large photo collections, and only a handful of apps can open SMB shares at all, most of them bloated. VLC comes closest, but it is a video player that happens to show photos too. DiaAbend is a photo viewer and nothing else.

<b>Features</b>
<ul>
<li>Finds SMB servers on the local network by itself (mDNS and a scan of the local subnet), or you enter the address</li>
<li>Guest access or login with user name and password, SMB 2 and 3</li>
<li>Browse shares and folders with the TV remote, start a slideshow of any folder</li>
<li>Slideshow with crossfades, every 10 seconds. Left/right: previous/next photo, OK: pause</li>
<li>Photos are shown upright (EXIF rotation) and scaled to the screen, so even 50 megapixel photos are no problem</li>
<li>JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, BMP, and HEIC on Android 9 or newer</li>
<li>Runs on Fire OS 5 and newer, Android TV, and also on phones and tablets (touch: tap left/right to switch photos)</li>
</ul>

<b>DigikamFS</b>
DiaAbend works especially well with DigikamFS (https://github.com/euphi/DigikamFS), which exports a digiKam photo collection as a share with downscaled copies, sorted by star rating. DiaAbend recognises this layout and skips the resolution and rating folders: you pick a resolution and a minimum rating once in the settings, and then just browse by year and album.

No ads, no tracking, no accounts. The app only talks to the servers in your own network.
